COMS 101 – Public Speaking (Section 07)
Instructor: Dr. Fischer Hewitt
Meeting Time: Weekly (see course schedule)
Format: In-person, participation-based

Course Overview:
This course develops foundational public speaking skills through three rounds of progressively complex speeches. Students will learn to structure arguments, conduct research, use visual aids, and deliver speeches confidently. A significant component of this course is peer evaluation — students will give and receive verbal and written feedback after every speech round. Active participation in evaluations is required for full credit.

Grading Breakdown:
  Homework and Classwork: 25 pts (Creating Classroom Community 15 pts, Office Visit 10 pts)
  Quizzes (4 total): 75 pts (Quiz 1–3: 15 pts each, Quiz 4: 30 pts)
  Specific Purpose and Central Idea Statements (3 total): submitted before each speech round
  Verbal Evaluations (peer feedback, multiple per round): 15 pts each
  Written Evaluations (3 rounds): 15 pts each
  Analyzing Delivery Style assignment: 30 pts
  Reflections (3 total): 10 pts each

Grading Scale: Standard (A 90–100, B 80–89, C 70–79, D 60–69, F below 60)

Weekly Schedule:

Week 1: Community Building
  - Introductions, course objectives, syllabus review, community agreements
  - Classwork: Creating Classroom Community (15 pts) — completed in class

Week 2: Speech Structure
  - Lecture: Introduction, body, conclusion; organizational patterns (chronological, topical, problem-solution)
  - Homework: Speech Structure reflection (ungraded practice)
  - Quiz 1 Sunday (Speech Structure, 15 pts, online)

Week 3: Delivery
  - Lecture: Eye contact, vocal variety, pacing, gestures, managing anxiety
  - Homework: Delivery reflection (ungraded practice)
  - Quiz 2 Sunday (Delivery, 15 pts, online)
  - Office Visit due this week (schedule a visit with instructor, 10 pts)

Week 4: Round 1 Prep — Introductory Speeches
  - Specific Purpose and Central Idea Statement due Wednesday (see assignment sheet)
    Submit: specific purpose (one sentence), central idea (one sentence), preview of 2–4 main points, one personal speaking goal for this round
  - Lecture: Tips for introductory speeches, Q&A

Week 5: Round 1 — Introductory Speeches (Part 1)
  - In-class speeches: teams deliver Round 1 introductory speeches
  - Verbal Evaluations: each student evaluates one team member's speech (15 pts, completed in class)
  - Written Evaluation Round 1 due Sunday (15 pts) — written feedback on a classmate's speech using course criteria: organization, delivery, content, eye contact, vocal variety

Week 6: Round 1 Speeches (Part 2) and Research
  - Remaining Round 1 speeches delivered
  - Lecture: Finding credible sources, integrating evidence, avoiding plagiarism
  - Homework: Research and source evaluation exercise (ungraded)

Week 7: Visual Aids and Round 2 Prep — Informative Speeches
  - Lecture: Designing effective slides and visual aids
  - Homework: Visual Aids design exercise (ungraded)
  - Quiz 3 Sunday (Research and Visual Aids, 15 pts, online)
  - Specific Purpose and Central Idea Statement — Round 2 due Sunday

Week 8: Round 2 — Informative Speeches
  - In-class speeches: teams deliver Round 2 informative speeches
  - Verbal Evaluations (15 pts each, completed in class)
  - Analyzing Delivery Style assignment due Sunday (30 pts) — watch a provided speech video, analyze the speaker's delivery using at least three concepts from course material, 400–600 words

Week 9: Persuasive Speaking and Round 3 Prep
  - Lecture: Aristotle's appeals (logos, ethos, pathos), persuasive organizational patterns (Monroe's Motivated Sequence)
  - Homework: Persuasive argument outline (ungraded)
  - Quiz 4 Sunday (Persuasive Speaking, 30 pts, online)
  - Specific Purpose and Central Idea Statement — Round 3 due Sunday
  - Written Evaluation Round 2 due Sunday (15 pts)
  - Reflection 2 due Sunday (10 pts) — one-sentence goal for Round 3, one paragraph plan for achieving it, 1–2 paragraphs reflecting on Round 2 execution and what you would change

Week 10: Round 3 — Persuasive Speeches
  - In-class speeches: teams deliver Round 3 persuasive speeches
  - Verbal Evaluations (15 pts each, completed in class)
  - Written Evaluation Round 3 due Sunday (15 pts)

Week 11: Finals Week
  - Reflection 3 due Sunday (10 pts) — final course reflection, 1–2 pages, double-spaced
  - No final exam
