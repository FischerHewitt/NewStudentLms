Overall I thought Jamie's introductory speech was a solid first round effort. There were clear strengths and a few areas that, with some targeted work, could make the delivery much more confident by Round 2.

In terms of organization, the speech had a recognizable structure — there was a clear opening that introduced the topic (growing up in a bilingual household), a body covering three distinct phases of Jamie's experience, and a conclusion that circled back to the opening image. The transitions between points were functional, though the one between the second and third main points was abrupt. Jamie moved directly from talking about middle school to describing a college visit without signaling that a shift was coming. A simple transition sentence — something like "That changed when I started thinking about college" — would have helped listeners follow the shift.

Delivery was conversational and mostly comfortable, but Jamie's pace was very fast in the first minute. The opening sentences came out in a rush, which made it harder to absorb the setup before the main content began. Around the two-minute mark the pace settled and the delivery became much more natural. I noticed Jamie slow down notably when describing the moment of speaking English for the first time at a family dinner — that contrast in pacing was effective and worth carrying into the whole speech.

Eye contact was one of the stronger elements. Jamie made deliberate eye contact with multiple sections of the room rather than staring at one side or reading from notes. There were a few moments where the gaze dropped to the note cards — specifically during the transition between points — but these were brief and did not break the connection significantly.

Vocal variety was present but somewhat inconsistent. The most effective moment was when Jamie raised volume and pace slightly while describing the argument with a sibling about speaking Spanish at home — the emotional energy came through. However, the closing sentence was delivered in a noticeably flatter tone, which undercut what should have been the strongest moment of the speech. Ending on a more intentionally rising or emphatic note would leave the audience with a stronger final impression.

On content, the speech was specific and personal, which is exactly right for an introductory speech. Jamie chose one coherent theme — navigating language identity — and stuck to it throughout. The personal examples were vivid and the speech felt honest rather than rehearsed. One suggestion: the third main point ended with a fairly general statement about the value of bilingualism that felt like a textbook claim compared to the rest of the speech. A specific detail or personal moment there would keep the speech consistent in its level of specificity.
