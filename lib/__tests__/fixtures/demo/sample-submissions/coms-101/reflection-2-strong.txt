My goal for Round 3 is to deliver the opening 30 seconds of my persuasive speech entirely without notes, making consistent eye contact with at least three different sections of the room.

To achieve this I am going to memorize the first minute of my speech word-for-word rather than just having a rough outline. In the past I have relied on note cards as a safety net even when I know the material, and the feedback I received after Round 2 was consistent: I look down too early. My plan is to practice the opening in front of a mirror five times before each rehearsal session this week, then deliver it to at least two people without any notes before the actual speech day. I will also record myself once so I can see objectively how much my gaze drops. If I still find myself reaching for the cards, I am going to physically set them on the table behind me during practice so I have to commit to memory.

Round 2 did not go the way I hoped, and being honest about why is uncomfortable. I had practiced the content well but I underestimated how much the visual aid would throw off my rhythm. I had never actually rehearsed with the slides live — I had practiced without them and assumed it would transfer. When the slide transitions did not match my pacing, I lost my place twice and the delivery became choppy in the middle section. The feedback I got confirmed that — two people mentioned the slide issues specifically. What I would do differently is run at least three full rehearsals with the actual slides before the speech so that the tech becomes automatic rather than something I am managing in real time. The content was there, the preparation just was not complete enough.
