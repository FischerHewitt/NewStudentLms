The sequences and series unit was more challenging than I expected, and the concept that gave me the most trouble was the ratio and root tests — not the mechanics themselves, but knowing which one to reach for first on a mixed problem. During the midterm I spent close to ten minutes on one problem because I kept second-guessing whether to use the ratio test or the limit comparison test. I eventually got the right answer, but I burned time I needed for later problems.

Thinking back, the issue was not that I did not understand the tests. I could reproduce each one from scratch and explain when they technically apply. The problem was that I had never practiced sorting a pile of unfamiliar series by type before deciding on a strategy. Every homework set was organized by test, so I never had to make that judgment call under time pressure.

For Midterm 2, I am making two concrete changes. First, I am going to build a decision flowchart for convergence tests — starting with the divergence test (always try this first), then checking whether the series is geometric or a p-series, then moving to ratio/root for anything with factorials or exponentials. I will tape it to my desk and quiz myself by covering it up and regenerating it from memory. Second, I am going to do at least two full timed practice sets each week using mixed problems from different sections rather than drilling topic by topic. The homework sets are organized too cleanly — real exam problems are not.

One thing that actually helped was study group work before this midterm. I had to explain why the radius of convergence for a Taylor series can be computed using the ratio test, and forcing that explanation out loud made me realize I had been doing it automatically without understanding it. I plan to do the same thing before every exam going forward — explain concepts out loud to someone else before I trust that I understand them.
